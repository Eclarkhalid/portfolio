SpyGlow is a competitive-intelligence product I founded and built to turn
changes on public competitor websites into evidence-backed actions. It is made
for founders, small teams and teams running several brands. (It was first
launched as CompetitorPulse.)

## What it does

- **One change, one action.** Each meaningful competitor change becomes a short
  summary, the reason it matters and a suggested action, delivered by email,
  Slack or webhook.
- **Section monitoring.** Watch the part of a page that matters, such as a
  pricing table, instead of every change on the whole page.
- **Every competitor surface.** Track a competitor's main site, docs, blog,
  changelog, status page and pricing together.
- **SEO audits against real rivals.** A structured SEO audit ranks your site
  against the competitors you actually track.
- **AI visibility.** See how your brand and competitors appear in answers from
  ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini, the layer that comes after search.
- **Battle cards and content gaps.** Battle cards and content-gap analysis are
  generated from the monitoring evidence.
- **AskGlow.** An AI analyst that answers questions grounded in your own
  monitoring data, built on a set of specialized AI agents.

## The product problem

Website monitoring is easy to describe and difficult to make trustworthy.
Pages fail to load, consent screens appear, navigation changes and minor markup
differences can look like meaningful business events. Sending every difference
to an AI model produces noisy alerts and unnecessary cost.

## The engineering approach

I designed the monitoring path as a layered pipeline. Capture-quality checks
reject weak snapshots, suspicious results are rechecked, and deterministic
comparison identifies real changes before AI analysis begins. Validated changes
can then feed summaries, alerts, reports, AskGlow and evidence-linked battle
cards.

The surrounding system coordinates scheduled monitoring, browser work, queues,
notifications and operational recovery. Each layer has a clear responsibility,
so AI adds judgment after the system has established reliable evidence.
