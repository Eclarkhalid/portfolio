### Strengths, in his words

- **End-to-end ownership.** He works across the stack, from front-end interfaces and back-end APIs to databases, integrations, deployment and production infrastructure. He cares whether the complete product works, beyond whether an individual feature is finished.
- **Practical problem-solving.** He focuses on the underlying problem and chooses an approach that fits the actual requirements, valuing useful solutions over unnecessary complexity.
- **Modern tools with engineering discipline.** He uses AI-assisted development and agentic workflows to speed up implementation and exploration, while keeping architecture, verification, security and maintainability central.

### A typical engagement

The process depends on the project. A typical product engagement runs:

1. **Understand the problem:** goals, users, constraints, existing systems and the definition of success.
2. **Define the approach:** scope, architecture, priorities, deliverables and milestones.
3. **Build incrementally:** the highest-priority functionality first, validating important assumptions and keeping progress visible.
4. **Review and verify:** demonstrate working software, gather feedback, test important flows and fix issues.
5. **Deploy and hand over:** the agreed solution with the documentation, configuration and deployment knowledge it needs.
6. **Support and improve:** ongoing maintenance, monitoring, fixes or further development, agreed separately.

He prefers small, reviewable milestones and working software over long stretches of development without visibility.

### Engineering opinions he holds firmly

- Understand the problem before choosing the technology. A fashionable stack is no substitute for sound product decisions.
- AI should accelerate engineering, never replace verification. Generated code still needs review, testing and validation.
- Deterministic checks should handle deterministic problems: tests, schemas, validation and explicit rules where predictable behavior matters. AI belongs where reasoning, interpretation or flexibility adds real value.
- Agents need boundaries: clearly defined responsibilities, constrained tools and approval requirements, especially for production changes.
- Security and maintainability are part of the product, from the first release.
- Architecture should be proportional to the problem, without abstractions or infrastructure ahead of their value.
- Production matters. A feature is finished when it works in production, beyond a local development environment.

He is interested in systems where conventional software engineering and AI complement each other.

For AI features he gives models clearly defined tools and responsibilities, uses structured interfaces and validates their outputs, instead of building an application around unconstrained model responses. These are preferences: the architecture should fit the product, team, operational requirements and budget.

## Facts: his story

### Path into software and products

Eclar is a software engineer from Kenya with a background in Business Information Technology. His work has grown across full-stack development, mobile applications, back-end systems, infrastructure and AI-powered products.

Building software for many different needs taught him to think beyond implementation: the user, the business problem, the technical trade-offs and the realities of operating software all matter. Building his own products extends that work. He can identify a problem, investigate it, form a product hypothesis, build a solution and learn directly from how people use it. That is why he builds products such as SpyGlow and Ryterr alongside his engineering work.

### Why he started SpyGlow

He started SpyGlow to change how businesses approach competitive research. Knowing that a competitor changed its website, published new content or sharpened its positioning is useful, but collecting those changes is only the beginning. The harder question is what the changes mean and what a business should do about them.

SpyGlow is built for competitive intelligence rather than simple competitor monitoring: it helps businesses understand what is changing across their competitive landscape, spot opportunities and make better-informed decisions. It brings together competitor monitoring, SEO intelligence, change analysis and AI-assisted insights. His longer-term direction is to make it more agentic, moving from reporting observations toward helping users interpret them and decide what to do next.

His guiding principle: **competitive intelligence, not competitor monitoring.** Website: https://www.spyglow.com

### Work he is proudest of

SpyGlow combines what he cares about most: a real business problem, full-stack engineering, AI integration and product thinking. Beyond an isolated AI feature, it is a whole intelligence product that collects information, identifies meaningful changes, interprets them and helps a user make a decision.

Ryterr explores how AI can support research-first content creation, giving the model enough context and evidence before it writes.

Both reflect the engineering he wants to keep doing: building complete products, testing ideas in the real world and improving them through iteration.
